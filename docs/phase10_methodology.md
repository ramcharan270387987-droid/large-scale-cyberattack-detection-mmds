# Phase 10 methodology

The phase10-v1 protocol preceded mining. Versions 1 and 2 verified full-corpus transaction construction and executed actual Spark MLlib FP-Growth with the first six registered numerical predictors. The 18-feature registry remains the transaction-audit contract. Versions 1 and 3 reuse the checksum-pinned Phase 2 version 1 feature-ready Parquet layer and exact Phase 4 B overlap-purging policy.

Version 3 exports complete counts of distinct six-item signatures by capture day, binary class, family and B split where applicable. This is an exact aggregation of all observations. Every itemset is a subset of a signature, so summing signature counts provides direct transaction support without revisiting raw flows. Family and day-specific frequent patterns are enumerated from these sufficient counts. Each signature contains one item per feature, which prevents incompatible feature bins within an itemset.

The primary three-bin full-corpus itemset registry is complete at 5% support (298 itemsets). Archived rule exports are bounded to 500 of 629 primary rules. All exported confidence, lift and joint-support values reconcile to complete itemset counts, while twenty canonical rules are independently reconstructed from signature counts. Confidence sensitivity enumerates singleton-consequent rules from the complete itemset collection. Leverage and conviction use their standard count-derived definitions. Infinite conviction remains explicit in JSON and undefined conviction is null.

Flow-weighted counts preserve all observations. Equal predictor-group weights divide each row's mass by its full-group multiplicity. Summed group mass can have floating-point roundoff; it represents approximately 10,368,838 groups in the full corpus. Domain support sensitivity uses the prespecified count and duration cuts and reference-thirds definitions for remaining features. Domain group-weighted mass was not exported.

Chronological pattern discovery uses only retained training observations. Eligible patterns have support at least 5%, attack count at least 100, attack/benign class-support ratio at least two and length two or three. Selection orders enrichment, attack support and canonical item IDs, then removes supersets with identical training support. Twenty patterns are retained. Validation chooses a whole-score threshold maximizing detected attacks under 1% benign FPR. The frozen JSON policy is written before test metrics are calculated. Three-bin and five-bin variants are evaluated separately, without revising the primary choice using test performance.

Temporal analysis fixes B training bin definitions, mines individual observed captures under those definitions, and tracks the bounded union of reference and day-specific patterns. Exact counts distinguish threshold crossings from genuine zero support. Capture dates are discrete observations with recorded calendar gaps. Class-conditional support and fixed-reference binary-mixture standardization are available only for supported strata. Jaccard similarity concerns the tracked pattern universe rather than the full space of every possible pattern.

Computational measurements concern private single-node Kaggle CPU execution. The original full-population timings include fitting and itemset-summary materialization. Bounded sample timings must be interpreted with their recorded sample sizes and timing scopes.
